01 · The role

A small company is not a simple company

López S.A. · Pension-contribution collections for the AFC and Chile’s leading AFPs · Santiago · Sep 2018 to Sep 2025

A ~30-person firm collecting, under legal mandate, the pension contributions that employers had left unpaid. A small niche with enormous density: every piece of data had an owner, a format, a deadline and a consequence.

Mine was a path, not a job title. I started on the administrative side of the operation, and the curiosity to understand every corner of the ecosystem kept pulling me toward management control: every corner understood became a proposal, every proposal a systematized process, and every systematized process something another person could run. That is how I arrived at the role I ended with.

In a large company this complexity is spread across departments. Here it converged on a single point, and that point was me. Not because I did everything, but because I was the one who understood how everything connected: what effect a court ruling had on the client remittance report, or a client’s format change on the internal system.

That is why my working pattern was this one, and it is the most transferable thing I have:

The density of the system

  • 4 institutional clients, each with its own format, channel, protocol and platform
  • 200+ types of procedural event in the life cycle of every court case
  • Tens of thousands of active cases in the portfolio at any given time
  • 3 kinds of deadline at once: those committed to each client, the procedural ones and the financial ones, with daily interest if the money stalls
  • A regulator (Chile’s Superintendency of Pensions) behind every client report

Analyze · Decode · Systematize · Control

My value was turning processes that depended on one person’s knowledge into systems that work without her. I kept for myself the part that cannot be systematized: the analysis, the design and the exceptions.

STEP 1

Analyze

Start from real data, not assumptions: explore the databases, cross-reference sources and measure, to understand what is truly happening before proposing anything.

STEP 2

Decode

Reverse-engineer the process: study the business rules, each client’s requirements and the mandated formats until the exact logic is extracted, with its conditions, joins and edge cases.

STEP 3

Systematize

Turn that logic into tools: ready-made queries, templates, step-by-step manuals. The process becomes runnable by anyone, with no prior knowledge and no risk of touching what is delicate.

STEP 4

Control

Progress indicators, quality controls and differentiated handling of exceptions. Deviations get caught fast and with data, before they escalate.

I connected: general management · the clients’ technical counterparts · systems development · attorneys and court agents · the administrative team · accounting and client remittances · the regulator, through the reports.

That cycle did include coordinating and handing off work, yes. But the heart was always the same: logic, data and design. And that mechanic travels to any context: if a process has rules, deadlines, data and exceptions, I know how to analyze it, decode it, systematize it and control it.

04 · Results

Three examples of my contributions and outcomes

There were many more. These three are the ones that fit best on a card.

AUTOMATION

Thousands of case files, one button

Assembling each case’s uncollectible-debt file was manual, document by document. I saw it could be systematized end to end: I translated the business logic into technical specifications and was the bridge to the development that implemented them.

From one by one, to thousands of cases per month. My specifications often traveled directly as SQL query logic, instead of traditional requirements documents.

OPTIMIZATION

A lighter caseload for everyone

The portfolio came to hover around 90,000 active cases. Combining the merger of cases against the same debtor, the reuse of analyses already done and the formal write-off of uncollectible cases, we reduced it steadily.

Hundreds of cases optimized every month, with documented savings in duplicated costs. Everybody won: the operation spent less, the debtor paid fewer court fees, and the team freed up time for the genuinely collectible cases, which were the goal.

WIND-DOWN

Closing well is also an achievement

In 2025, a legal change led the company to wind down. The technical closure of the three contracts fell to me: reconciling the portfolio, documenting every delivery and handing it over to the clients in order. They kept me on for two additional months as an external consultant to secure the transition.

Three portfolios delivered fully reconciled. With one client: 60,000+ records at a 99.3% reconciliation match.
